Life Insurance — Plain Language

Understand it before you decide anything.

Most people avoid this conversation because the industry made it complicated. It isn't. Here's what you need to know.

What it does

A contract that replaces your economic contribution when you can't.

You pay premiums. In return, the insurer pays a lump sum — the death benefit — to the people you designate if you die while the policy is in force. That payment is generally tax-free.


The goal isn't to replace you. It's to replace your income, your mortgage payments, your children's tuition — long enough for the people you love to find solid footing.


$1,400 Average monthly Social Security survivor benefit — less than most mortgages
Types of coverage
Term Life Pure protection · Most affordable

Coverage for a fixed period — 10, 20, or 30 years. If you die during the term, the benefit is paid. No cash value, no complexity. The right starting point for most families who need maximum coverage at minimum cost.

Best for: Young families, mortgage holders, primary earners needing income replacement during earning years.

Whole Life Permanent · Builds cash value

Coverage that lasts your entire life, combined with a savings component that grows at a guaranteed rate. Premiums are fixed. Cash value can be borrowed against tax-free. A financial asset, not just protection.

Best for: Estate planning, wealth transfer, those who want permanent coverage with a guaranteed savings component.

Indexed Universal Life Flexible · Market-linked growth

Cash value tied to a market index — typically the S&P 500 — with a floor that protects against losses. Flexible premiums and death benefits. Complex when structured poorly; powerful when structured correctly.

Best for: Business owners and high earners seeking tax-advantaged accumulation alongside permanent protection.

Disability Income Income protection · Often overlooked

Replaces a portion of your income if illness or injury prevents you from working. For most people under 60, the probability of a disabling event is significantly higher than premature death. This is frequently the most important and least-purchased coverage in any plan.

Best for: Independent contractors, self-employed individuals, anyone without employer disability benefits.
