Life Insurance — Plain Language

Understand it before you decide anything.

Most people avoid this conversation because the industry has made it unnecessarily complicated. It isn't. Here's what you actually need to know.

The basics

What life insurance actually does.

A life insurance policy is a contract. You pay premiums — monthly or annually — and in return, the insurer agrees to pay a lump sum to the people you designate if you die while the policy is in force. That payment, called the death benefit, is generally tax-free to whoever receives it.


That's the core of it. Everything else — the type of policy, the amount, the term length — is about making sure the contract actually fits your situation. A policy that doesn't fit is almost as bad as no policy at all.


The goal isn't to replace you. It's to replace your economic contribution — the mortgage payment, the school tuition, the income your family depends on — long enough for the people you love to find solid footing.

Types of coverage

Term Life

Pure protection

Coverage for a defined period — typically 10, 20, or 30 years. If you die during the term, the benefit is paid. If you outlive it, the policy ends. No cash value, no investment component, no complexity. It is the most affordable form of life insurance and the right starting point for most people who are primarily concerned with income replacement during their earning years.


Best for: Young families, mortgage holders, primary earners with dependents who need maximum coverage at minimum cost.

Whole Life

Permanent + cash value

Coverage that lasts your entire life as long as premiums are paid, combined with a savings component that builds cash value over time. Premiums are higher than term but fixed for life. The cash value grows at a guaranteed rate and can be borrowed against — tax-advantaged — for any purpose. A whole life policy is a financial asset, not just a protection instrument.


Best for: Those who want permanent protection, estate planning tools, or tax-advantaged wealth transfer to the next generation.

Indexed Universal Life

Flexible + market-linked growth

A form of permanent life insurance where the cash value component grows based on the performance of a market index — typically the S&P 500 — with a floor that protects against losses. You can participate in market upside without direct market exposure. Premiums and death benefits are flexible within limits. IUL is complex but powerful when structured correctly for the right client.


Best for: Business owners, high earners seeking tax-advantaged accumulation, and those who want permanent coverage with growth potential above what whole life offers.

Disability Income

Your income is the asset

Not life insurance — but closely related. Disability coverage replaces a portion of your income if illness or injury prevents you from working. For most people under 60, the probability of a disabling event is significantly higher than the probability of premature death. If your income is what your family depends on, its interruption is the real risk. This is often the most important and most overlooked coverage in any financial plan.


Best for: Independent contractors, self-employed individuals, anyone without employer disability benefits — particularly healthcare workers and athletes.

$1,400 Average monthly Social Security survivor benefit
The gap most people don't know exists

Most people assume Social Security will provide meaningful support for their family if they die. It will provide something — but the average survivor benefit is around $1,400 a month. If your family's mortgage alone exceeds that, the math doesn't work.


Life insurance exists to close that gap. The question isn't whether you need it — it's how much, what kind, and for how long. Those answers depend entirely on your specific situation, which is why we don't quote before we listen.
